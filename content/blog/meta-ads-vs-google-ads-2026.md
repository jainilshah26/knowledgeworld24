"Should I run Meta or Google ads?" is one of the most common questions we get from businesses starting to scale paid acquisition — and the honest answer is that it depends on what you're solving for.

## Google Ads: capturing existing demand

Google Ads works because it meets people already looking for a solution. Someone searching "AI marketing agency Ahmedabad" already knows they have the problem and is actively evaluating options. Google Ads puts you in front of that intent at the exact moment it exists.

This makes it the stronger choice when:

- You have a well-defined, searched-for product or service
- Your sales cycle rewards being found at the moment of intent
- You can tolerate a higher cost-per-click in exchange for higher purchase intent

## Meta Ads: creating new demand

Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads work differently — they interrupt, not respond. Nobody is searching for your product on Instagram; you're introducing them to a problem they may not have realised they had, using creative that stops the scroll.

This makes it the stronger choice when:

- Your product benefits from visual storytelling
- You're targeting a broader audience that doesn't yet know your brand
- You want lower-cost reach to build a retargeting pool for later

## The real answer: run both, differently

The businesses that scale fastest rarely pick one platform — they use Google to capture people who are ready now, and Meta to build awareness and warm up everyone who isn't ready yet. The split in spend should follow your funnel:

| Funnel stage | Platform | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Meta | Reach, low-cost impressions |
| Consideration | Meta retargeting | Warm audience nurture |
| Intent | Google Search | Capture active searchers |
| Retargeting | Both | Close warm leads |

Most businesses under-invest in the awareness stage and wonder why their Google costs keep climbing — without Meta building a pipeline of warm prospects, Google ends up fighting for cold traffic at premium prices.
